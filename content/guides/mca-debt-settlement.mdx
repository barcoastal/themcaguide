<KeyTakeaways items={["Settlement requires an agreement with the party entitled to resolve the obligation; a provider's forecast is not an accepted offer.", "Measure savings after every fee and required payment, using a verified starting balance.", "The written release, payment conditions, guarantor treatment, and lien handling matter as much as the dollar amount."]} />

MCA settlement is a negotiated agreement resolving an advance-related obligation on specified terms. It may involve a discount, installments, a release, or other conditions. There is no universal percentage that every business can obtain. The agreement, payment history, available cash, disputed issues, and the funder's position all matter.

<h2 id="net-savings">Calculate net savings, not the advertised discount</h2>

**Original hypothetical example:** a funder claims an $80,000 balance. A written proposal would resolve it for $48,000. A provider would charge $8,000 and counsel would charge $2,000. Total outlay would be $58,000, leaving $22,000 of savings compared with the claimed balance, or 27.5%.

The headline reduction from $80,000 to $48,000 is 40%, but that is not the net saving after fees. Neither percentage establishes that the claimed balance is correct or the proposal will be accepted. Verify the balance first and include ongoing debits, account charges, litigation expenses, or other amounts required by the arrangement.

Use the [debt relief cost calculator](/tools/mca-debt-relief-cost-calculator) as an illustration, then replace every assumption with written figures. If a provider charges on enrolled debt rather than savings, ask how the fee changes when a balance is disputed or a funder refuses to negotiate.

<h2 id="affordability">Can you actually fund the agreement?</h2>

A discounted settlement can still fail if its payment schedule is unrealistic. Match each due date against expected cash receipts and essential expenses. Ask whether a missed installment cancels the discount, restores a larger balance, triggers additional charges, or has other legal consequences. Have counsel explain the specific language.

If a proposal requires a lump sum, identify its source before committing. Taking a new high-cost advance to fund settlement may leave the business with another unaffordable obligation. Compare the complete replacement arrangement using the [repayment-trouble framework](/guides/mca-debt-relief-2026-guide).

<h2 id="written-terms">A checklist for the written terms</h2>

- **Correct parties:** the business, funder or current holder, and any guarantors are accurately identified.
- **Covered agreements:** every account, renewal, and disputed obligation being resolved is listed.
- **Payment terms:** amounts, dates, permitted payment methods, and conditions are unambiguous.
- **Release:** identify who is released, from which claims, and when the release becomes effective.
- **Pending proceedings:** specify what happens to lawsuits, judgments, restraints, and enforcement activity where applicable.
- **Security interests:** address relevant financing statements, liens, and termination or release documents.
- **Failure scenario:** understand late-payment, default, and reinstatement provisions.

This is a discussion checklist, not a ready-to-sign legal document. A release covering only the business may leave a guarantor exposed depending on its terms. A promise to discuss a lien later is different from a clear obligation to provide appropriate release documents.

<h2 id="ucc">Payment and a UCC record are separate checks</h2>

Do not assume a public financing statement disappears automatically when funds are sent. Track the filing number and the documentation needed to address it. For example, [New York UCC § 9-513](https://www.nysenate.gov/legislation/laws/UCC/9-513) imposes termination duties under specified conditions; the provision is not a blanket rule that every MCA filing must vanish immediately after a payment.

Our [UCC filing guide](/articles/mca-ucc-filing-explained) explains what to collect and why the collateral and governing law matter. Ask counsel to connect the settlement terms to the actual filings.

<h2 id="provider">What to ask the person negotiating</h2>

Who will contact the funder? Who holds any money you deposit? Are funds available to you if negotiations fail? What work does the fee buy, and when is it earned? Does the engagement include defense of a lawsuit? Who is the lawyer, if any, and who is that lawyer's client?

A sales representative's statement that attorneys are available is not the same as an engagement with an attorney representing your company. Verify representation and scope using the [attorney selection guide](/guides/mca-attorney-complete-guide).

<h2 id="after-settlement">After the last payment</h2>

Save the executed agreement, proof of every payment, written confirmation of completion, applicable releases, and any filed dismissal or termination documents. Check the records the agreement required to be updated. Ask your accountant whether the transaction has tax or accounting consequences for the business or owners.

If the available offers do not fit your cash flow, revisit other options instead of treating settlement as the only route. A modification, disputed-balance review, or broader restructuring discussion may address a different part of the problem.
