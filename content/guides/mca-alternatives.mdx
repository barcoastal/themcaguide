The most useful alternative to an MCA depends on what the money must accomplish. A customer paying late, a machine that needs replacing, and a business losing money each week are three different problems. Treating each as an urgent need for the same short-term product can make the cash shortage harder to solve.

<h2 id="define-the-gap">First, define the gap</h2>

Write down the amount needed, the deadline, the expected source of repayment, and how certain that source is. Separate a one-time gap from a recurring loss. If normal sales do not cover ordinary expenses, new financing adds another obligation unless something else changes.

**Example:** a $12,000 payroll gap caused by a $20,000 customer invoice due next month is a timing problem. A $12,000 payroll gap that returns every month despite timely customer payments calls for a review of margins, expenses, pricing, and business viability. The funding amount is identical; the appropriate response may not be.

<h2 id="options">Five routes worth comparing</h2>

### A bank or credit-union line of credit

Ask about a revolving line when cash needs fluctuate. Compare interest, unused-line or maintenance fees, collateral, guarantees, renewal conditions, and whether the lender can reduce availability. A credit limit is not the same as cash you can draw unconditionally. Bring current financial statements and existing financing agreements to the conversation.

### An SBA-backed loan

The [SBA 7(a) program](https://www.sba.gov/loans/7a-loans/) supports qualifying business financing through participating lenders. Working capital and some debt refinancing are among permitted uses, subject to program requirements and underwriting. Do not assume an existing MCA qualifies for refinance or that an SBA application will solve a deadline this week. Ask the lender specifically about your use of proceeds, existing obligations, and expected decision time.

### A smaller loan through an intermediary

The [SBA microloan program](https://www.sba.gov/loans/microloans/) provides loans of up to $50,000 through nonprofit intermediaries. SBA says proceeds cannot be used to pay existing debts or purchase real estate. That makes it a possible avenue for an eligible operating need, but not an automatic MCA payoff solution. Intermediaries set their own credit requirements and terms within the program.

### Invoice financing or factoring

If other businesses owe you money for delivered goods or services, ask whether eligible invoices can support financing. Examine the advance percentage, reserve, fees that accumulate while invoices remain unpaid, customer notification, recourse, and existing lien restrictions. Obtain an illustration for both an on-time and a late customer payment. A quote of a small weekly fee can grow significantly when an invoice stays unpaid.

### Operational changes

Request a customer deposit for new work, accelerate invoicing, follow up on overdue accounts, negotiate supplier timing, or defer a discretionary purchase. These options depend on counterparties agreeing and may involve discounts or business costs. They are worth calculating alongside borrowing, rather than assuming that financing is the only available response.

<h2 id="compare-offers">Use the same comparison sheet for every offer</h2>

For each option record: cash received today; every scheduled payment; upfront and recurring fees; collateral and guarantees; early-payoff terms; time to funding; and what happens if receipts fall. Ask the provider to clarify any blank field.

**Original comparison example:** Offer A delivers $48,000 after withheld fees and requires $67,500 in payments. Offer B delivers $50,000 and requires $59,000. The cost above cash received is $19,500 for A and $9,000 for B. That does not finish the comparison: a shorter repayment window for B could still create a larger weekly cash burden. These are hypothetical offers, not representative market prices.

Use the [MCA calculator](/tools/mca-calculator) for an advance, then review the [MCA-versus-loan guide](/guides/mca-vs-business-loans) for contract differences. Keep quotes dated because terms and eligibility change.

<h2 id="existing-mca">If you already have an MCA</h2>

Disclose it when applying for financing. Obtain a written payoff statement and a list of liens, guarantees, and outstanding commitments. Ask the new provider whether refinancing replaces the old obligation completely or merely adds another withdrawal. Calculate net new money after all payoffs and fees.

If you cannot afford current payments, start with the [repayment-trouble guide](/guides/mca-debt-relief-2026-guide) before accepting another advance. Replacing one daily debit with a smaller debit is useful only if the complete arrangement is sustainable.

<h2 id="first-action">A practical first action</h2>

Prepare your latest bank statements, profit-and-loss statement, balance sheet, receivables aging, and a list of current obligations. Ask at least two suitable providers for written terms based on the same need. A business counselor can help you organize the comparison; legal questions about existing contracts belong with qualified counsel.
