<KeyTakeaways items={["Start with every agreement, an accurate balance, and a short-term cash forecast.", "Reconciliation, payment modification, refinancing, settlement, and legal defense solve different problems.", "A debt-relief company cannot promise that a funder will accept an offer or that a lawsuit will stop."]} />

If MCA withdrawals are squeezing payroll, inventory, or rent, the first goal is a reliable picture of the problem. A sales pitch about cutting a percentage of the balance is not a plan. You need to know what is owed, what can be paid, what contractual options exist, and whether a legal deadline is approaching.

This guide is an organizational framework. Contract enforcement, business-debt protections, and court procedures vary by jurisdiction and facts. If you have been served with a lawsuit, received a restraint notice, or face a court deadline, bring the documents to qualified counsel promptly.

<h2 id="inventory">Build one inventory of every obligation</h2>

For each agreement, record the legal funder name, original advance, cash received, purchased amount, payments made, current claimed balance, debit frequency, collateral, guarantors, and contact details. Attach amendments, renewals, payment histories, and any broker correspondence.

Reconcile the funder's claimed balance against your records. A bank statement proves a debit occurred; it may not show how the funder allocated it. Request an itemized payment history and a dated payoff figure. Keep disputed fees separate from amounts you agree are accurate.

<h2 id="forecast">Work out what the business can actually pay</h2>

Prepare a weekly cash forecast using cash expected to arrive, not merely invoices issued. List essential operating expenses and all financing payments. Build a lower-sales scenario. The [cash-flow calculator](/tools/cashflow-calculator) can help with a first pass, but a detailed forecast should include actual due dates.

**Illustrative example:** after ordinary expenses, your business has $3,200 per week before financing. Two advances collect $450 and $250 each business day, totaling $3,500 over five days. You are already $300 short before a sales decline or unexpected expense. A workable proposal must address the total burden, not just reduce one debit while adding a new one.

<h2 id="options">Understand the options before comparing providers</h2>

### Reconciliation under the existing agreement

If the agreement provides a way to align estimated withdrawals with actual receipts, locate that provision. Ask what documents are required, the calculation method, the response timeframe, and whether any adjustment is prospective or retrospective. Send the request through the specified channel and preserve confirmation. A request alone does not mean the payment has changed.

### A negotiated payment modification

You can ask for a different schedule or temporary accommodation. Acceptance is not guaranteed. Evaluate the complete written amendment: total payments, added fees, maturity or purchased amount, releases, and default consequences. A smaller debit may extend the obligation or increase its total cost.

### Settlement

Settlement involves an agreed resolution of an obligation, sometimes for less than the claimed balance. The funder must agree. Savings must be measured after provider fees, legal costs, and any other required payments. Use our [settlement guide](/guides/mca-debt-settlement) to review the arithmetic and documents.

### Refinancing

A replacement facility is useful only if you qualify and the combined transaction is affordable. Include the old payoff, new charges, and net money received in your comparison. Do not assume all [SBA 7(a) debt-refinancing uses](https://www.sba.gov/loans/7a-loans/) are eligible for your specific agreement. A participating lender must evaluate program and underwriting requirements.

### Legal review or insolvency advice

Counsel can evaluate disputed balances, guarantees, collection methods, contract classification, defenses, and possible restructuring or bankruptcy routes. A settlement salesperson is not a substitute for representation in a pending case. Use the [attorney guide](/guides/mca-attorney-complete-guide) to prepare for a consultation.

<h2 id="screen-providers">Questions for a debt-relief provider</h2>

Ask who will perform the work, whether an attorney is engaged, who represents you if litigation starts, and what is excluded. Get the fee formula, payment dates, cancellation policy, refund terms, and control of any settlement account in writing. Ask what happens if no funder accepts a proposal.

Treat guaranteed savings, guaranteed lawsuit protection, or a claimed government MCA forgiveness program as statements requiring independent verification. The FTC warns about guarantees in [consumer debt-relief marketing](https://consumer.ftc.gov/consumer-alerts/2024/03/carrying-credit-card-debt-how-avoid-debt-relief-scams). That consumer guidance is a useful warning-sign reference, but do not assume consumer-debt rules automatically apply to commercial MCA obligations.

<h2 id="avoid-new-risk">Avoid creating a second problem</h2>

Do not make a blanket decision to stop payments, close accounts, redirect receipts, or ignore legal papers based on a website or sales call. Ask counsel about the consequences under your actual agreements before changing payment arrangements. Continuing an unaffordable debit also has consequences; the point is to evaluate both with complete information.

<h2 id="next-24-hours">A useful preparation checklist</h2>

Gather every contract, recent bank statements, the debit ledger, the next payroll and tax dates, and any notices. Write a one-page timeline. Identify the immediate cash shortfall and the earliest legal deadline. Then choose the appropriate next conversation: the funder's reconciliation team, a qualified attorney, your accountant, or an alternative lender.

You do not need a guaranteed outcome to take a useful first step. You need a complete set of facts and a proposal grounded in what the business can sustain.
