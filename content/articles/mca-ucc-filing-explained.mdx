<KeyTakeaways items={["A UCC financing statement is a public filing related to a claimed security interest or another covered transaction; it is not itself a court judgment.", "Read the filing together with the agreement and collateral description.", "Payoff and termination are separate steps. Applicable duties depend on the transaction, governing law, and statutory conditions."]} />

An MCA provider may file a financing statement connected to the transaction. Owners often call this a “UCC lien,” but the filing alone does not explain every right the provider has. Start with the filed record, the signed agreement, and the collateral description.

<h2 id="record">What the record can tell you</h2>

A financing statement generally identifies a debtor, a secured party, and collateral. The UCC framework sets rules for sufficiency and effectiveness; [UCC § 9-502](https://www.law.cornell.edu/ucc/9/9-502) describes basic financing-statement requirements. The law enacted in the relevant state, rather than a generic summary, controls a particular dispute.

A record is not proof that the provider owns every asset or can immediately empty an account. It is also not a court judgment. Attachment, authorization, perfection, priority, and enforcement are different legal questions. If someone is threatening collection, give counsel both the threat and the underlying documents.

<h2 id="collect">Collect a complete record, not just a search result</h2>

Use the appropriate official filing office and the exact legal debtor name. Obtain the initial financing statement and available amendments, continuations, assignments, or terminations. Record the filing number, filing date, debtor name, secured party, and collateral language.

A search under a trading name may miss a filing under the entity's legal name. A result for a similar business may concern someone else. Ask the filing office or counsel about the correct search method and jurisdiction rather than assuming your operating state is always the right location.

<h2 id="example">A practical review example</h2>

Imagine your company received an advance from “Example Funding LLC,” but the public record lists a different secured party. That does not automatically make the record improper: there may have been an assignment, servicing arrangement, or a separate entity in the contract. Put the names side by side and ask for documentation connecting them.

Likewise, a broad collateral description in the public filing should be checked against the signed security agreement. Write down the discrepancy without assuming the filing or the contract can be ignored. This organized comparison is useful to counsel and to a prospective lender reviewing existing obligations.

<h2 id="payoff">What to check when the advance is paid or settled</h2>

Request a dated payoff statement and written confirmation when the required payment clears. For a negotiated resolution, ensure the agreement addresses the appropriate release or termination documents and identifies the relevant filings. A balance paid to one provider does not prove that every obligation covered by a filing has ended.

The [model UCC § 9-513](https://www.law.cornell.edu/ucc/9/9-513) distinguishes consumer goods from other collateral and includes conditions for termination duties. As a state example, [New York § 9-513(c)](https://www.nysenate.gov/legislation/laws/UCC/9-513) refers to a 20-day period after a signed demand when specified conditions apply. It is not a universal “20 business days after MCA payoff” rule. Sales of accounts have their own conditions within the section.

Have counsel determine which provision applies, what demand is needed, who must act, and whether you have received an effective release. Do not file a termination you are not authorized to file.

<h2 id="future-financing">Why this matters for new financing</h2>

A new lender may ask about existing filings before extending credit. Keep your agreements, payment records, payoff letters, and release documents together. Explain unresolved records upfront. Different lenders have different requirements; the existence of a filing does not by itself establish that every future financing option is unavailable.

If you are comparing new funding, read [MCA alternatives](/guides/mca-alternatives). If a balance is disputed, use the [attorney checklist](/guides/mca-attorney-complete-guide). If you are negotiating payoff terms, review the [settlement guide](/guides/mca-debt-settlement) before focusing only on a discounted number.

<h2 id="questions">Questions to resolve before closing the file</h2>

- Does the legal debtor name match the company?
- Do the filing and agreement identify the same transaction and relevant collateral?
- Is there an assignment or amendment that explains a different secured party?
- Have all obligations covered by the release actually been satisfied?
- Who must deliver or file which document, and when?
- Has the required public record update occurred, and have you saved proof?

For an active dispute, those questions are a starting point for legal review, not a conclusion about priority or enforceability.
